Why I built HEAR

Experience

I often missed what people said. When teachers or friends called my name, I sometimes didn’t hear them, so I couldn’t answer, and sometimes it looked as if I was ignoring them on purpose. Without my hearing aid, when someone speaks on my left side, I can’t hear anything at all. In the worst moments, I answered without knowing what had been said, because I felt bad asking again.

Noticing

I realized that going along with a conversation I hadn’t understood, just to avoid asking again, wasn’t fair to the person talking to me, and it meant I was missing what was actually happening.

Question

That left me with a question: how can communication still go well in situations where I can’t hear?

Research

I researched the communication challenges people with hearing loss face, and tested how accurately speech-to-text captions work in English and Korean under different levels of background noise. That showed me what HEAR needed to solve.

Building

HEAR is what I built. Live Hear turns speech into text in real time, so when I miss something, I can follow along with the captions. Sign to Text came from another question: how can people who use sign language and people who don’t communicate with each other? For many Deaf people, sign language is their primary language, but people who don’t sign often can’t understand it. Sign to Text aims to help by turning signs into written text. Because I am not Deaf, I opened its sign library so that people who sign can add their own signs and help decide what it should recognize.

Don't just ask, "How well can you hear?" Ask, "How well can the world communicate with you?"

What HEAR is

A platform, not a diagnosis

HEAR is a web-based accessibility platform inspired by real experiences of hearing loss. Rather than treating hearing loss as a single medical condition, HEAR is designed around the everyday communication environments in which people experience barriers — classrooms, restaurants, meetings, and conversations with the people closest to them. The platform combines an interactive listening assessment, personalized accessibility recommendations, real-time captions, and anonymous user insights.

HEAR is not a medical device and does not diagnose hearing loss. If you have concerns about your hearing, consider consulting a qualified hearing professional.
